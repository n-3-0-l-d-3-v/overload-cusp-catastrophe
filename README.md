## What the model says

A latent functional-load coordinate `x` relaxes in a cusp potential
`V(x;a,b) = x⁴/4 − a x²/2 − b x`. Demand sets the asymmetry `b`; depleted
regulatory capacity sets the splitting factor `a`. The claim is that capacity
does not add to load — it changes the geometry. Above a critical `a` the system
acquires a second attractor, a fold, and a memory.

Everything else follows rather than being assumed:

| Phenomenon | Status in this model |
|---|---|
| abrupt onset | fold bifurcation |
| delayed recovery | hysteresis, width `= (4/3√3)·a^{3/2}` |
| critical slowing down | `λ → 0` at the fold, theorem not postulate |
| the state "stuck" | lower basin while an overloaded attractor coexists |
| the state "recovering" | upper basin though demand already fell below entry |
| transition matrix | generated by Kramers rates from 9 parameters, not 20 counts |

## Pre-stated predictions

- **P1** hysteresis width ∝ `a^{3/2}`
- **P2** `Var ∝ μ^{−1/2}` and `−log AC1 ∝ μ^{+1/2}` in distance `μ` to the fold
- **P3** overload dwell times over-dispersed vs geometric
- **P4** re-entry hazard decays at rate `ε`
- **P5** larger `α_A` ⇒ longer recovery

These are exponents, not directions. They can fail, and they did.

## What was actually found

Three results, and the two that matter most are about method rather than
physiology.

**1. The standard early-warning estimator returns the wrong sign.** On series
simulated from a model that genuinely contains a fold, rolling-window
autocorrelation and variance recovered exponents of the wrong sign in **12 of
12** configurations (windows 30/60/120, with and without detrending, `n` from
1,500 to 20,000), while the theory reproduced its own exponent (+0.565 vs
+0.50 predicted). Near the fold the relaxation time exceeds the window, so the
indicator saturates exactly where the effect is largest. Our own P2 test is
therefore withdrawn as uninterpretable — and so, arguably, is a good deal of
published practice.

**2. The nominal significance test is inflated ~7×.** On near-unit-root series
like tonic EDA, the nominal test for the cubic coefficient rejects roughly a
third of random walks instead of 1 in 20. Surrogate calibration fixes it.

**3. A well-powered null.** Across 157 recordings from three corpora, no cusp
signature above the calibrated null. Calibrated rejection rate 6.2% at 30 s vs
5% expected by chance, stable across sensor configurations and analysis
windows. Power to detect the effect was ≈1.00, so this is a null, not a
non-result.

A cautionary detail: the marginal distribution of the load coordinate *is*
decisively bimodal against a random-walk null, while the dynamics contain no
cubic term at all. Reporting only the distributional evidence would have
produced the opposite conclusion.

## Scope

Motivated by sensory–executive overload in autistic adults. **No autistic
participants are analysed**; no claim about autistic adults is made from these
data. No public wearable dataset of autistic adults exists at the required
density — see `data/DATASETS.md`. A pre-registered validation protocol is
specified in the paper.
